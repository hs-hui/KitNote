# KitNote: what differs from MuseScore Studio

KitNote is MuseScore Studio **v4.7.5** (tag `v4.7.5`, commit `3654226c`) changed into a drum-notation editor. The engraving engine, the file format and the way of editing are MuseScore's. What KitNote takes away, what it adds (drum patterns, sticking, repeat signs, two palettes, a metronome option for audio export) and the three places where playback differs (ghost notes, grace notes, hi-hat signs) are listed below.

Every change in the source is marked with a `KitNote:` comment. `git diff v4.7.5` shows all of them.

| **Drum score from a recording** (File menu, a card on Home, a recording dropped on the window, the MCP tool `transcribe_audio`): the drums are separated from the other instruments by Demucs (htdemucs); engine 2 (`engine.json` version 2) finds beats and downbeats with Beat This! (CPJKU, MIT), moves them onto the strokes (the models mark a stroke 20-30 ms apart), and takes the strokes from KitNote's own drum model (`share/ai/kitnote_drum_model.py`, weights `kitnote_drums.pt`: convolutions and a GRU over a log-frequency spectrogram of the drum stem, five classes; trained by `tools/kitnote/train_drum_model.py` only on data whose licence allows an open-source model (no non-commercial terms) - the Groove MIDI Dataset, a part of E-GMD, grooves of 26 styles made by `random_grooves.py`, played by `render_kits.py` on 51 openly licensed sampled kits (acoustic, brushes, 808/909 and electronic), and some of them put into songs and separated again by Demucs (`separated_stems.py`); see `share/ai/kitnote_drums.NOTICE.txt`) or, without it, from the band analysis; what a model does not tell is measured on the separated drums (open/closed hat and hat or cymbal per measure, crash or ride, tom pitch, ghost notes by loudness against the strong snares) - engine 1, the fallback: their strokes found by the jumps of four frequency bands and sorted (bass drum, snare, three toms by pitch (a low tom struck with the snare by its ring at 110-150 Hz after the snare has gone), closed and open hi-hat, crash), the beat tracked by dynamic programming, hi-hats open or closed and time on the hi-hat or on a cymbal decided per measure (by how long the hats ring, and how bright the strokes struck alone are), weak snare strokes left out unless `--ghost-notes` (tuned against a published chart of a real song: bass drum 0.99, snare 0.92, hi-hat 0.81, cymbal 0.78), the time signature found from the groove when not given (`--meter auto`: 3/4 when it repeats every three beats, 6/8 when the dotted-quarter beat divides in three and repeats every two); engine 2 takes the beats to a measure from the downbeats, mends a beat the beat model heard twice or missed, and tells 6/8 and 12/8 by the strokes: beats grouped in threes by the bass drum and the snare (the model counted the eighths), or beats divided in three with the middle eighth struck (a shuffle leaves it out and stays 4/4); where each measure begins in the recording (`measure_times`, for the hidden tempo marks) is read off the line through the strokes on one side of a sudden change of tempo, not a line through a few strokes on both; the hum of the mains (50/60 Hz and harmonics, found as needles in the quietest seconds, measured by the turn of their phase) is notched out, half a hertz wide, before the strokes are looked for, and a tom the model hears where only a steady tone rises is left out (`hum_removed` in the result), and the strokes written on a grid of sixteenth notes - or of triplet eighths in a measure whose strokes fall on thirds of the beat (a shuffle, a triplet fill) - in the time signature chosen (4/4, 3/4, 2/4, or 6/8 and 12/8 with a dotted-quarter beat of three eighths or six sixteenths), the downbeat where bass drum and snare usually fall; sections are marked where the groove changes and stays changed (A, B, A ...), and repeated measures can become % signs. The engine - uv, Python 3.11, PyTorch (CPU), Demucs and its model, about 1 GB - measured on a rendered 83-measure chart mixed with bass and chords (3 min 24 s, separated in about a minute on the processor), stroke by stroke (F): bass drum 0.98, snare 0.92, hi-hat 0.81, crash and ride 0.68, toms 0.46. With the drum model (2.5 million parameters, trained on about 420 hours), against the published chart of a real pop-rock song from its full mix: bass drum 0.99, snare 0.94, hi-hat 0.96, cymbals 0.98, open or closed hat right 99 %; on a drums-only recording with fills and toms: all strokes 0.97 precision, 0.96 recall (bass drum 0.98, snare 0.98, hi-hat 0.94, cymbals 0.94, toms 0.94). It is installed into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\KitNote\ai` only after the user agrees in a dialog that says what is downloaded from where (Preferences > AI, or the audio dialog), and removed there again. The dialog's tempo field shows the note it counts in MuseScore's musical font, as a tempo marking does: `♩`, or `♩.` in 6/8 and 12/8. | `share/ai/kitnote_audio.py`, `share/ai/install_engine.ps1`, `aipreferencesmodel.cpp` (`AiEngine`, `AudioToScoreModel`), `AudioToScoreDialog.qml` |

## What is not changed

- The file format is MuseScore's. A drum score written by KitNote opens in MuseScore Studio (after renaming `.kitnote` to `.mscz`), and a percussion-only MuseScore score opens in KitNote.
- Internal names were renamed, so the source no longer carries the upstream names: the C++ namespaces `mu` and `muse` are now `kn` (engraving and app layers) and `kfw` (framework); the engraving class `MScore` is `EngravingGlobals`; the macros `MUSE_APP_*` are `KITNOTE_APP_*`, other `MUSE_*` are `KFW_*`, and `MUE_*`, `MUSESCORE_*` and `Mscore_*` are `KITNOTE_*`; the CMake functions and targets `muse_*` are `kfw_*`; the QML modules `MuseScore.*` and `Muse.*` are `KitNote.*`; the URI scheme `musescore://` (and `muse://`) is `kitnote://`; the translation catalogues `musescore_<lang>.ts` are `kitnote_<lang>.ts`.
- Some names are kept on purpose, so that existing files, settings and sound profiles still load: the `museScore` root element and the `programVersion` of score files; the `.mscz`, `.mscx`, `.mss`, `.mpal` and `.drm` suffixes and the MIME type `application/x-musescore`; the font families stored in scores (MuseJazz, Leland, Edwin, Bravura, MScore, the MuseScore Tab fonts ...); the sound-profile ids `MuseScore Basic` and `MS Basic`; the MEI setting `export/mei/useMuseScoreIds`; and the MusicXML, MEI and MuseData format names.

## Checking a build

```bash
python tools/kitnote/smoke_test.py
```

runs the installed `KitNote.exe` from the command line and checks its version, the `.kitnote` round trip, every export format, that ghost notes are quieter and the open sign changes the hi-hat sound in exported audio, what the drum commands write (through `KITNOTE_TEST_APPLY`, a hook in the converter that applies them to a score before it is written), and that a score with a pitched instrument is refused.

```bash
python tools/kitnote/stress_test.py
```

takes about seven minutes: every drum pattern and rudiment on every bundled template, chained with counting, the drum key, repeat signs, sticking and their opposites, to find crashes and commands that do not undo each other.

```bash
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\kitnote\test_install.ps1
```

tries `install.ps1` (install, reinstall, refusals, uninstall) against a scratch folder and a scratch registry key, and removes both again.

## Building on Windows

Requirements: Visual Studio with the C++ desktop workload, CMake, Ninja, Git for Windows, Qt **6.10.2** (`msvc2022_64`) with the modules `qt5compat qtnetworkauth qtshadertools qtwebsockets`. Nothing else has to be unpacked by hand: CMake downloads the few build-time dependencies it needs during configure. They are listed, with their licences, in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#build-time-downloads).

Qt can be installed with [aqtinstall](https://github.com/miurahr/aqtinstall):

```bash
python -m aqt install-qt windows desktop 6.10.2 win64_msvc2022_64 -m qt5compat qtnetworkauth qtshadertools qtwebsockets -O C:\Qt
```

Generate the drum SoundFont once (needs the full `share/sound/MS Basic.sf3`, which is in the repository):

```bash
python tools/kitnote/make_drum_soundfont.py "share/sound/MS Basic.sf3" "share/sound/KitNote Drums.sf3"
```

Then, from a "x64 Native Tools Command Prompt" with Qt's `bin` on `PATH` and `CMAKE_PREFIX_PATH` pointing at the Qt folder:

```bash
set KITNOTE_RUN_WINDEPLOYQT=ON
```

```bash
set KITNOTE_APP_BUILD_MODE=release
```

```bash
bash ./ninja_build.sh -t install
```

The application is installed to `build.install/bin/KitNote.exe`.

`ninja_build.bat` has Unix line endings in a Git checkout without `core.autocrlf`, and `cmd` does not run it in that state; call `ninja_build.sh` through Git Bash as above.

## Packaging

```bash
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\kitnote\package_windows.ps1
```

writes `dist/KitNote-<version>-win64.zip` (about 69 MB). The zip is portable: unpack it anywhere and run `bin\KitNote.exe`.

To install it instead, run this in the unpacked folder:

```bash
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File install.ps1
```

It copies KitNote to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\KitNote`, adds a Start menu shortcut (`-DesktopShortcut` adds one on the desktop too), opens `.kitnote` files with KitNote, and adds KitNote to "Installed apps", from where it can be removed. No administrator rights are needed, and `install.ps1 -Uninstall` removes everything except scores and settings. `register_filetype.ps1` only makes the file association, for a portable copy (`-Remove` undoes it).

The drum SoundFont was checked against the full one by exporting the same score to audio with each: the two WAV files are identical sample for sample.
